IEEE 802.11r · Cross-Band Fast BSS Transition

How a cross-band fast roam talks

The client roams from a 5 GHz BSS to a 6 GHz BSS in the same mobility domain. The frames are the ordinary FT exchange — band is not a protocol parameter — but watch two things: the key hierarchy holds a separate PMK-R1 per band's R1KH, and the client's radio has to leave its serving band at a different moment for over-the-air than for over-the-DS.

Grounded in the wiki's 802.11r — Fast BSS Transition page, §9 Multi-band (cross-band) roaming, and the FT packet walkthrough. The two APs may equally be two radios of one physical AP; what matters is they share the MDID and each band's radio is its own R1KH holding its own PMK-R1 off the single PMK-R0. The OCI subelement (Operating Channel Validation) authenticates which band/channel the roam landed on. Backplane hops (fetching the target band's PMK-R1 from the R0KH) are the transport the standard leaves out of scope — the classic R0KH_UNREACHABLE failure. Field lists are representative, not byte-exact.
