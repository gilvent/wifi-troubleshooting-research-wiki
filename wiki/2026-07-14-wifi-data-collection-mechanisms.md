# WiFi Troubleshooting Data Collection — Industry Mechanisms

*Survey of how commercial AI/cloud WiFi-troubleshooting systems and open telemetry standards actually collect their data: what is measured, from which vantage point, over what transport, and — critically for this wiki's research direction — when collection escalates.*

> Sources: vendor documentation and datasheets (Juniper Mist, Cisco Meraki, Cisco Catalyst Center, HPE Aruba Central/UXI, Extreme CloudIQ, Nile, 7SIGNAL, Wyebot, ThousandEyes), open specs and reference implementations (Wi-Fi Alliance Data Elements Jan-2024 overview, Broadband Forum TR-369/TR-181/TR-157, OpenSync `src/sm`, TIP OpenWiFi `PROTOCOL.md`, prplMesh `ap_metrics.md`), and [the research direction](edge-diagnostic-agent-research-direction.md) §3.1, §5, §9 for framing. The passive-sniffer mechanism (§6) draws on Cisco's 802.11 sniffing fundamentals (doc 200527) and Catalyst 9800 Sniffer Mode guide (doc 217057), HPE Aruba AOS-8 AP packet-capture CLI, LiveAction/Omnipeek, the WLAN Pi user guide and WLANPiShark2, Intuitibits (Airtool/WiFi Explorer remote sensors), the Wireshark WLAN capture wiki, Meraki's wireless-capture analysis guide, mrn-cciew, and an IEEE multi-channel-capture paper. Compiled 2026-07-14 (mechanisms 1–6, transport, escalation) and extended 2026-08-07 (§6 independent sniffer node) via parallel, adversarially-verified web-research passes; per-claim URLs inline. Unverifiable claims are flagged in §8.

## 1. The collection mechanisms

Industry systems combine five core mechanisms (the table below), plus two that borrow a vantage the serving AP does not own: the client's radio via 802.11k, and a fully independent passive sniffer. Almost every product is a particular mix of these.

| # | Mechanism | Vantage point | Airtime cost | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Infrastructure event/stat streaming** — AP/controller continuously exports connection events + RF counters | AP serving radio, controller | ~zero (side effect of serving) | Mist ap-stats every 2 s; Meraki Health |
| 2 | **Dedicated scanning radio** — extra radio on the AP does passive scans, spectrum, WIPS | AP, non-serving radio | zero on serving channel | Mist 3rd/4th radio; Meraki Air Marshal radio; Nile 3rd radio |
| 3 | **Triggered deep capture** — packet capture armed by anomaly detection | AP (serving radio for OFDMA correctness) | storage/uplink, not airtime | Mist dynamic PCAP; Meraki Proactive PCAP; Cisco iCAP |
| 4 | **Synthetic testing** — a real or emulated client runs active tests (assoc/auth/DHCP/DNS/HTTP/VoIP) | Dedicated sensor, AP-as-client, or agent on endpoint | **real airtime + association slot** | 7SIGNAL Sapphire Eye; Aruba UXI; Marvis Minis; Nile sensors |
| 5 | **Client-side telemetry** — SDK/agent on the actual user device reads OS radio state | The client itself | ~zero (passive) or low (agent probes) | Mist Marvis Client + Zebra; ThousandEyes Endpoint Agent; 7SIGNAL Mobile Eye |

A sixth quasi-mechanism rides inside 802.11 itself: **802.11k beacon reports** let the AP ask an associated client to scan and report what *it* sees — client-perspective RF data with no installed agent ([Cisco](https://blogs.cisco.com/networking/why-the-802-11k-and-neighbor-report-are-important); EasyMesh wraps the same primitive in its Beacon Metrics Query, [prplMesh #399](https://github.com/prplfoundation/prplMesh/issues/399)). This is the only agentless path to the client's view, and it costs the client an on-air scan.

A **seventh mechanism** sits outside the infrastructure entirely: the **independent passive sniffer node** — a separate physical device (a dedicated appliance, an AP repurposed into sniffer mode, or a laptop/SBC in monitor mode) that passively records raw over-the-air 802.11 frames from a third-party vantage. Unlike the six above, it is owned by neither the serving AP nor the client, transmits nothing, and can be placed next to the roaming client to capture what that client actually hears. It is the classic Wireshark/Omnipeek troubleshooting method and the DIY analog of mechanism 3, and it is detailed in [§6](#6-the-independent-passive-sniffer-node-the-seventh-mechanism).

## 2. What is collected (the common data model)

Across vendors and standards the collected entities converge on the same shape — Radio → BSS → associated STA, plus neighbor scans and events:

- **Client connection state machine** — per-client association → authentication → DHCP → DNS/ARP steps with per-step timing and failure reason. This is the backbone of every AIOps product: Mist tracks "100+ states per device" feeding its SLE classifiers ([Mist SLE](https://www.juniper.net/documentation/us/en/software/mist/mist-wireless/shared-content/topics/concept/service-level-expectations.html)); Meraki Health scores the same four onboarding steps ([Meraki Health](https://documentation.meraki.com/Platform_Management/Dashboard_Administration/Operate_and_Maintain/Monitoring_and_Reporting/Meraki_Health_Overview)); Catalyst Center scores onboarding per phase (assoc/AAA/DHCP).
- **Roaming events** — roam success rate, roam latency (Mist targets ~200 ms for 802.11r vs ~2 s full roam), old-AP-dissociation-to-first-packet-on-new-AP delta (Meraki).
- **RF counters** — per-STA RSSI/SNR as seen by the AP, PHY rates, retries/errors; per-radio channel utilization split into own-BSS / other-WiFi / non-WiFi (exactly the `Radio.Transmit`/`ReceiveSelf`/`ReceiveOther` split in [Wi-Fi Data Elements](https://www.wi-fi.org/system/files/Wi-Fi_CERTIFIED_Data_Elements_Technology_Overview_202401.pdf)).
- **Neighbor/channel scans** — off-channel or scanning-radio surveys of neighboring BSSs and per-channel utilization.
- **Packet captures** — management/EAP/DHCP frames around failures (see §4); Mist explicitly never collects payloads, "only transmission and connection data" ([Mist pcap doc](https://www.juniper.net/documentation/us/en/software/mist/mist-wireless/shared-content/topics/topic-map/packet-captures.html)).
- **Application/flow telemetry** — on-AP DPI (Aruba AppRF, Meraki L7), NetFlow/IPFIX; used to link "the call broke" to the flow that broke.
- **Synthetic-test KPIs** — time-to-connect per step, ping/HTTP/throughput, VoIP MOS/jitter/loss (7SIGNAL layers 2–7 tests; UXI DHCP/DNS/portal/app timings).
- **Client-perspective data** (only where a client SDK exists) — device-measured RSSI, adapter/driver version, WiFi↔cellular handoffs, and **the client's own roam decisions** — Mist frames the Marvis Client explicitly as visibility into "client roaming algorithms since roaming is a client decision" ([Marvis Client](https://www.juniper.net/documentation/us/en/software/mist/mist-aiops/topics/concept/marvis-client-overview.html)).

## 3. Transport: how data reaches the analysis backend

- **Persistent TLS channel on 443 is the norm** for enterprise WLAN: Mist uses HTTPS to `ep-terminator.mistsys.net` with ap-stats every 2 s ([Mist cloud connectivity](https://www.mist.com/documentation/ap-connectivity-to-the-mist-cloud/)); Aruba Central uses a WebSocket; Meraki a proprietary AES-256 tunnel carrying protobufs, ~1 kbps/device idle management traffic ([Meraki](https://documentation.meraki.com/General_Administration/Other_Topics/Management_traffic_for_Cisco_Meraki_devices)); Extreme uses DTLS-secured CAPWAP.
- **MQTT is the de facto bus for ISP/home CPE**: OpenSync pushes protobuf stat reports over MQTT via its Queue Manager ([OpenSync whitepaper](https://discover.plume.com/rs/776-GZM-466/images/Plume-Opensync_for_the_connected_home.pdf)); Wyebot sensors upload metadata over MQTT/TLS ([Wyebot FAQ](https://wyebot.com/platform/faq/)); TR-369/USP lists MQTT, WebSocket, STOMP, CoAP as standard bindings ([TR-369](https://www.broadband-forum.org/pdfs/tr-369-1-3-0.pdf)).
- **On-prem/cloud splits exist where privacy demands it**: Catalyst Center collects on-prem via gRPC/gNMI model-driven telemetry from the 9800 WLC, then exports de-identified data over HTTPS to the Cisco AI cloud, opt-in ([Cisco admin guide](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/td/docs/cloud-systems-management/network-automation-and-management/catalyst-center/3-2-x/admin-guide/cisco-catalyst-center-admin-guide-3-2-x/configure-external-services/t_configure_cisco_ai_network_analytics_data_collection.html)).
- **Every mature stack ends up hybrid push+pull**: periodic streaming for cheap stats, subscription/notify for events, on-demand query for deep data. USP replaced TR-069 polling with Notify subscriptions but kept Get; uCentral has periodic `state`/`healthcheck` messages plus a `telemetry` command that starts a 0–60 s-interval stream which **auto-stops after 30 minutes** ([uCentral PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/Telecominfraproject/wlan-cloud-ucentralgw/blob/main/PROTOCOL.md)); EasyMesh has on-demand metric queries *and* interval reporting *and* threshold-triggered push, configured by a Metric Reporting Policy TLV ([prplMesh ap_metrics](https://github.com/prplfoundation/prplMesh/blob/master/documentation/flows/agent/ap_metrics.md)).
- **Remote-capture export is a distinct transport class for the sniffer mechanism** (§6): a passive sniffer node rarely stores pcap locally — it streams frames to the analyst's machine. Aruba AOS-8 encapsulates over-the-air frames in a UDP header for a remote Omnipeek/Wireshark analyzer (Wireshark's Aruba ERM dissector, [Aruba](https://arubanetworking.hpe.com/techdocs/CLI-Bank/Content/aos8/ap-pkt_cpt.htm)); the WLAN Pi streams via Wireshark's `sshdump` extcap or WLANPiShark2's `tcpdump`-over-SSH pipe ([WLANPiShark2](https://github.com/WLAN-Pi/WLANPiShark2)). This is a point-to-point analyst pull, not the persistent-cloud push of mechanisms 1–5.
- **Telemetry buffering during WAN outage is a consistent documentation gap** — vendors document that the data plane survives cloud loss, none publish back-fill behavior.

## 4. Escalation: triggered deep collection (the load-bearing section)

The industry already runs a **two-tier sensing architecture**: cheap always-on telemetry plus expensive deep capture that is *gated*. Three vendors automate the escalation:

- **Mist dynamic PCAP** — any client connection failure "automatically triggers a short-term dynamic packet capture" stored in the cloud; trigger list includes DHCP timeout/denied, RADIUS failures, association failure, and notably **11r FBT failure** and OKC auth failure; retained 7 days ([dynamic pcap](https://www.mist.com/documentation/dynamic-packet-captures/), [trigger list](https://www.juniper.net/documentation/us/en/software/mist/mist-wireless/shared-content/topics/topic-map/packet-captures.html)).
- **Meraki Proactive PCAP** — fires on connection failure **or roaming issue**; rate-limited to the first failure per 5-minute interval; 7-day retention ([Meraki pcap](https://documentation.meraki.com/Platform_Management/Dashboard_Administration/Troubleshooting_and_Support/Troubleshooting/Packet_Capture_Overview)).
- **Cisco Intelligent Capture (iCAP) Anomaly Capture** — always-on per-AP capture of management frames for clients with onboarding issues, on a direct AP→Catalyst-Center gRPC channel (TCP 32626) bypassing the WLC; repeated identical anomalies are **aggregated at the AP into one event before sending**; hard caps: 100 packets per onboarding event, 1 GB rolling full-capture, 14-day retention ([iCAP](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/td/docs/cloud-systems-management/network-automation-and-management/catalyst-center-assurance/2-3-7/b_cisco_catalyst_assurance_2_3_7_ug/b_cisco_catalyst_assurance_2_3_6_ug_chapter_01110.html)).

Aruba's AI Assist auto-collects logs+pcaps on infrastructure events (port/tunnel flaps) and pre-populates TAC cases; UXI hardware sensors run automated follow-up triage tests when a synthetic test fails ([UXI](https://help.capenetworks.com/en/articles/11504575-how-uxi-helps-identify-and-troubleshoot-network-and-application-issues)). Extreme and Nile document no automatic capture — deep collection is manual/on-demand.

The rate limits, dedup, and byte caps above are **hardcoded cost-control policies**: evidence that vendors treat deep sensing as budgeted, but the budget logic is static (first-failure-per-5-min, fixed packet caps), not a learned or information-driven policy.

## 5. Sensor vs. infrastructure vs. client-agent trade-offs

- Dedicated sensors (7SIGNAL Sapphire Eye, Wyebot, UXI, Nile wall-plug) run true end-to-end tests from a fixed vantage point — association, 4-way handshake, DHCP, DNS, HTTP, VoIP MOS — on fixed cadences (UXI ~3 min/network; Nile every minute backing a financially-guaranteed SLA, [Nile SLAs](https://docs.nilesecure.com/nile-slas)). They consume real airtime and an association slot; **no vendor publishes a quantified airtime-overhead figure for its own active tests**. The closest admissions: 7SIGNAL markets its sensor's *Ethernet* backhaul as "zero impact to the Wi-Fi environment" ([7SIGNAL](https://7signal.com/platform/sensors/)), and Cape/UXI concedes its sensor's own capture buffer depresses its measured throughput ([UXI FAQ](https://help.capenetworks.com/en/articles/3295755-faq-s)).
- Wyebot's answer to measurement interference is hardware: 3 radios — one active-testing as a client, two purely passive capturing 24/7 with 24-hour pcap retention on the sensor itself ([Wyebot](https://wyebot.com/blogs/complete-packet-capture-support/)). Radio separation removes self-corruption of the measurement but not contention with production traffic on the tested channel.
- Sensors acting "as a client" reveal only how *the sensor's chipset* would roam. Only software on the real device (Marvis Client, Zebra Wireless Insights, 7SIGNAL Mobile Eye, ThousandEyes Endpoint Agent) sees genuine roam decisions — and this category is mostly **passive** (reads OS/driver state, near-zero airtime; Marvis Client quantifies cost as ~2–3%/hr battery, [FAQ](https://www.juniper.net/documentation/us/en/software/mist/mist-aiops/topics/concept/marvis-client-faq.html)). ThousandEyes documents its endpoint collection precisely: continuous WiFi state (RSSI, BSSID, retransmission rate, roaming events) plus per-minute ICMP/TCP probes to gateway/proxy/VPN, all over HTTPS 443 ([ThousandEyes](https://docs.thousandeyes.com/product-documentation/global-vantage-points/endpoint-agents/how-endpoint-agents-work/how-does-the-endpoint-agent-work.md)).
- Client agents remain rare in enterprise WLAN — Mist is the only WLAN vendor with a first-class client SDK; everyone else infers client experience from the infrastructure side, or borrows the client's radio via 802.11k beacon reports.

## 6. The independent passive sniffer node (the seventh mechanism)

The six mechanisms in §1 all read a vantage the network owns — the serving AP, its scanning radio, a vendor sensor, or the client's own OS/radio. The seventh reads from outside that entirely: a **separate physical device dedicated to passive over-the-air 802.11 capture**, placed where neither the AP nor the client sits. A monitor-mode radio "stop[s] transmitting and receiving any of its own data" ([Meraki](https://documentation.meraki.com/Wireless/Troubleshooting_and_Support/Troubleshooting/Analyzing_Wireless_Packet_Captures)), so its airtime cost is **truly zero** — the only mechanism in the survey for which that is literal, not approximate. And because monitor mode disables SSID filtering, it records **every** management, control, and data frame of **every** SSID on the tuned channel at full radiotap/PHY detail (retries, PHY rates, RSSI) — a superset of any single serving-BSS or client-scoped capture ([Wireshark WLAN wiki](https://wiki.wireshark.org/CaptureSetup/WLAN)).

Its decisive property for roaming work is **placement**. Put the sniffer physically next to the roaming client and the trace approximates "what [the] client device hears over the air" ([Cisco doc 200527](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/support/docs/wireless-mobility/80211/200527-Fundamentals-of-802-11-Wireless-Sniffing.html)) — the client's RF reality, with no agent on the client. Cisco states the vantage contrast plainly: a sniffer near the infrastructure "report[s] the 'point of view' of the static APs rather than the client," whereas to see the RF as the client sees it "a multi-channel wireless trace can be captured with a laptop with multiple Wireless NICs" carried near the client ([200527](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/support/docs/wireless-mobility/80211/200527-Fundamentals-of-802-11-Wireless-Sniffing.html)). The two vantages are complementary, so best practice for a roaming case is **two simultaneous captures** — one AP-side, one over-the-air near the client ([200527](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/support/docs/wireless-mobility/80211/200527-Fundamentals-of-802-11-Wireless-Sniffing.html); [mrn-cciew](https://mrncciew.com/2024/10/16/wi-fi-pcap-with-ekahau/)).

### 6.1 Implementations

- **An AP dedicated to sniffer mode.** A Cisco Catalyst 9800 AP placed in **Sniffer Mode** becomes a pure capture device: switching modes reboots the AP and "an access point configured as a sniffer cannot simultaneously provide wireless access service"; it captures on a single primary channel and width the operator must set to match the client, and forwards captured frames to a remote analyzer ([Cisco doc 217057](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/support/docs/wireless/catalyst-9800-series-wireless-controllers/217057-configure-access-point-in-sniffer-mode-o.html)). *(The commonly-cited export detail — a specific UDP port and PEEKREMOTE dissector — did not survive verification; treat only "forwards to a remote analyzer" as established.)* HPE Aruba AOS-8's AP **packet-capture** is the same pattern with documented transport: it "encapsulate[s] [the WiFi packets] in a UDP header and sen[ds] to a client running a packet analyzer like ... Airopeek, Omnipeek, or Wireshark," decoded via Wireshark's Aruba ERM dissector ([Aruba AOS-8](https://arubanetworking.hpe.com/techdocs/CLI-Bank/Content/aos8/ap-pkt_cpt.htm)). Both sit at the *infrastructure* vantage — an AP-side sniffer, not a near-client one.
- **A dedicated multi-radio appliance.** The **Ekahau Sidekick 2** carries 4 tri-band (2.4/5/6 GHz) radios and captures 4 channels **simultaneously and continuously**; select more than 4 channels and the radios fall back to **dwell-time hopping**, reopening the single-channel gap where off-channel frames (e.g. beacons on a channel not currently tuned) are missed ([mrn-cciew](https://mrncciew.com/2024/10/16/wi-fi-pcap-with-ekahau/)). The **WLAN Pi Pro** is a single-board computer with two monitor-capable Wi-Fi modules used as a standalone remote node ([WLAN Pi user guide](https://userguide.wlanpi.com/wlan-pi-os/packet-capture)).
- **A software analyzer fed by dedicated USB adapters.** **Omnipeek/LiveAction** ships its own MediaTek-driver capture adapters (a 3-stream 802.11n and a 2-stream 802.11ac model) plus an **Aggregator/Roaming adapter** that merges several channels into one capture window to measure roam latency across APs ([LiveAction](https://mypeek.liveaction.com/driver_downloads.php)). On the open-source/macOS side, **Wireshark** and Adrian Granados' **Airtool 2 / WiFi Explorer Pro** drive one or more monitor-mode adapters directly.

### 6.2 Techniques

- **Single-channel lock** — the baseline: pin the radio to the AP's exact channel and width and *validate* the capture landed there; never leave it in scan/hop mode ([Cisco 200527](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/support/docs/wireless-mobility/80211/200527-Fundamentals-of-802-11-Wireless-Sniffing.html); [Meraki](https://documentation.meraki.com/Wireless/Troubleshooting_and_Support/Troubleshooting/Analyzing_Wireless_Packet_Captures)).
- **Multi-adapter multi-channel rig** — one radio hears one channel at a time ([Wireshark WLAN](https://wiki.wireshark.org/CaptureSetup/WLAN)), so capturing a roam across channels needs several adapters, classically three on **channels 1/6/11** for 2.4 GHz ([Cisco 200527](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/support/docs/wireless-mobility/80211/200527-Fundamentals-of-802-11-Wireless-Sniffing.html)), or the Sidekick's four radios.
- **Remote-capture export** — the sniffer node rarely stores locally; it streams frames to the analyst's machine (see §3): Aruba's UDP-encapsulated ERM to Omnipeek/Wireshark ([Aruba](https://arubanetworking.hpe.com/techdocs/CLI-Bank/Content/aos8/ap-pkt_cpt.htm)); the WLAN Pi via Wireshark's `sshdump` extcap or **WLANPiShark2**, which runs `tcpdump` on the Pi and pipes it over an SSH/Plink connection into a local Wireshark ([WLANPiShark2](https://github.com/WLAN-Pi/WLANPiShark2)); or a **Raspberry Pi 4** with an external monitor-capable USB adapter acting as a remote sensor for WiFi Explorer Pro 3 / Airtool 2 ([Intuitibits](https://www.intuitibits.com/2021/02/17/using-a-raspberry-pi-4-as-a-remote-sensor-for-wifi-explorer-pro-and-airtool/)).
- **Pcap aggregation/merge** — captures from several radios or sensors are merged into one timeline by packet timestamp (Omnipeek's Aggregator/Roaming adapter; Airtool 2 merges two or more capture sources in real time by timestamp, [Intuitibits](https://www.intuitibits.com/2021/02/02/multi-channel-captures-in-airtool-2/)).

### 6.3 What it buys for roaming / 802.11k/v/r

An over-the-air capture near the client records the frames a roam is actually made of: the **reassociation request** the client emits at the new AP ("mainly useful in client roaming situations," [Meraki](https://documentation.meraki.com/Wireless/Troubleshooting_and_Support/Troubleshooting/Analyzing_Wireless_Packet_Captures)), the full 802.11 authentication/association exchange, and the **EAPOL 4-way handshake** — plus, on-channel, the 802.11k beacon-report responses, 802.11v BTM action frames, and 802.11r FT exchange that an infrastructure-side view may only partially show. Academic work confirms the multi-monitor approach can be made efficient — intelligent channel-switching across a few monitors reached >70% capture, 200–300% better than random hopping — but that figure is from a single paper for **legacy 802.11b/g only** and must not be generalized to modern PHYs ([IEEE multi-channel-capture paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251882090_Multi-Channel_Packet_Capture_in_80211bg_Wireless_Networks)).

### 6.4 Limitations

- **Single-channel blindness** — one radio, one channel; channel hopping "will inevitably cause you to lose traffic" ([Wireshark WLAN](https://wiki.wireshark.org/CaptureSetup/WLAN)). A roam that crosses channels is only fully seen with enough radios to cover every channel involved.
- **Channel-width / spatial-stream gaps** — a sniffer AP captures only its **primary** channel ([Cisco 217057](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/support/docs/wireless/catalyst-9800-series-wireless-controllers/217057-configure-access-point-in-sniffer-mode-o.html)); an 80/160 MHz transmission, or one using more spatial streams than the sniffer's radio chains, is partially missed. *(How much is lost is not well quantified in the sources — see §8.)*
- **No decryption without keys** — the sniffer sees ciphertext; decoding data payloads needs the PSK/PMK plus a captured 4-way handshake, and 802.11r FT / WPA3-SAE complicate key derivation ([mrn-cciew decrypt walkthrough](https://mrncciew.com/2014/08/16/decrypt-wpa2-psk-using-wireshark/)). Management/control frames are in the clear regardless (until 802.11w protects some of them).
- **Time-sync across sniffers** — a remote-capture timestamp is "the time that the packet is received by the client [analyzer] ... not synchronized with the time on the AP" ([Aruba](https://arubanetworking.hpe.com/techdocs/CLI-Bank/Content/aos8/ap-pkt_cpt.htm)); merging multiple independent sniffers into one roam timeline needs external clock discipline the tools don't provide by default — see §8.
- **SKU churn** — specific gear ages faster than the method: LiveAction's 802.11n adapter carries an End-of-Sale notice and the `wifiexplorer-sensor` project is deprecated (the capability persists via Airtool Pi). The *mechanism* is stable; the parts list is not.

This is the mechanism the wiki's own [home-lab testbed](home-wifi-roaming-scenario.md) already uses in miniature — its "monitor capture" node is a single-channel instance of exactly this.

## 7. Relevance to the edge-diagnostic-agent research direction

Mapping this survey onto [the research direction](edge-diagnostic-agent-research-direction.md):

- **The two-tier structure already exists** (§4) — always-on cheap telemetry + gated deep capture is standard practice. What does not exist anywhere in the survey is a *policy* deciding escalation: triggers are hardcoded event lists (Mist's failure enum, Meraki's 5-min dedup). This is direct evidence for the gap claimed in §9 of the direction: "commercial systems do not treat monitoring cost as a decision variable at all."
- **Retention windows quantify RQ1's irreversible information loss**: Mist pcaps 7 days / telemetry 60-day rolling; Meraki client RSSI **2 hours**, proactive pcaps 7 days; Cisco anomaly pcaps 1.05 GB FIFO. A post-hoc query outside these windows is unanswerable by construction — the industry has already committed to retention policies, just not learned ones.
- **The cost model's structure (direction §5) is corroborated by hardware**: vendors physically separate sensing from serving (scanning radios, Wyebot's radio split, 7SIGNAL's Ethernet backhaul) precisely because passive monitoring on a serving radio and on a dedicated radio have categorically different costs — the capability-on-hardware pairing argument.
- **Nobody publishes the airtime cost of their own active testing** (§5). The direction's inflicted-harm axis has no commercial precedent to reuse; that measurement has to be built.
- **The passive sniffer node (§6) is the one existing non-participating observer** the survey turns up — a zero-airtime, zero-transmit vantage that meets the direction's non-participating-observer requirement (direction §6) literally. But it is a *manual* tool: an engineer places it, locks its channel, and reads the pcap after the fact. There is no automated triggering, no learned channel/placement policy, and its own limitations (single-channel coverage, cross-sniffer time-sync, no decryption without keys) are exactly the problems an automated edge observer would inherit. It is the closest off-the-shelf analog to the direction's ideal observer, and the gap between it and that ideal is the same gap §4 identifies for escalation — the mechanism exists, the policy driving it does not.
- **802.11k beacon reports are the in-protocol version of RQ3's dilemma**: the AP spends client airtime (an on-air scan) to buy client-perspective evidence — a per-probe cost/information trade the standards expose but no surveyed system optimizes. EasyMesh's Metric Reporting Policy TLV (interval + RCPI/utilization thresholds) is the closest standardized knob an escalation policy could actuate.
- **Precedents for edge-side data reduction** (Cisco's AP-side anomaly aggregation, uCentral's auto-stopping telemetry stream, USP's subscription model) are the mechanism layer the direction's §5 explicitly defers — they exist off the shelf; the policy that drives them is the open ground.

Only "the sniffer AP forwards captured frames to a remote analyzer" is established ([Cisco 217057](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/support/docs/wireless/catalyst-9800-series-wireless-controllers/217057-configure-access-point-in-sniffer-mode-o.html)).
